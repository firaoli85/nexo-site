# PROVIDER REQUIREMENTS RESEARCH — DC / Maryland / Virginia

**Task:** P1b (SITE_GROUND_TRUTH §6). **All research access dates: 2026-08-17.** Branch `v2`.

## Purpose

Primary-source truth for how a transport company **becomes and stays** a compliant NEMT provider in the
District of Columbia, Maryland, and Virginia. This feeds the V2 provider resource center
(`/providers/requirements`, `/providers/credentialing`, `/providers/{dc,maryland,virginia}`, and downloadable
guides). The industry lane (`docs/DESIGN_RESEARCH.md`) established the bar — Verida's published checklist,
credentialing sequence and insurance specificity; MTM's state provider manuals. **This document gets the real
requirements from the real authorities.**

## SOURCE LAW (absolute)

- Every requirement is labelled **VERIFIED** (primary source: state agency page, regulation text, official
  manual, or a broker's official provider page — with URL and access date) or **REPORTED** (secondary or
  unclear) or **`UNRESOLVED`** (looked for, not found — with an account of where the search went).
- **Contradictions between sources are recorded, never resolved by guessing.** Several appear below, including
  one inside MTM's own DC materials.

### b. Becoming a provider — the path

1. **1. Form the DC entity and appoint a registered agent** — Register the corporation/LLC/partnership (and any trade name) with DLCP through CorpOnline/BOSS. "All businesses must have an Agent who can receive legal documents for your business. This person must be a DC resident. You may serve as your own Agent." Sole proprietors with no trade name may skip entity registration.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: DLCP Steps to Obtaining a Basic Business License https://dlcp.dc.gov/node/1618236 (loaded 2026-08-17); DLCP Basic Business License Application (fillable PDF) https://dlcp.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dlcp/publication/attachments/BBL%20Application%20(Fillable).pdf (downloaded, text-extracted 2026-08-17)
2. **2. Get an EIN and register for DC taxes** — Obtain a federal EIN from the IRS, then register it with the DC Office of Tax and Revenue at mytax.dc.gov (or in person at 1101 4th Street SW, 2nd Floor).
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://dlcp.dc.gov/node/1618236 (loaded 2026-08-17)
3. **3. Get zoning clearance: Certificate of Occupancy or Home Occupancy Permit** — A Certificate of Occupancy is required for a commercial location; a Home Occupation Permit if you operate from a residence. The BBL application asks for the permit number and issue date. This is where a vehicle-parking/dispatch yard question usually bites an NEMT operator, and DC's guidance does not address vehicle storage.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://dlcp.dc.gov/node/1618236 and the BBL Application PDF (both loaded 2026-08-17)
4. **4. Pass Clean Hands** — CONTRADICTION IN DC'S OWN DOCUMENTS - record both. The BBL Application PDF states: "The District Government will not issue or reissue any license or permit if the applicant owes more than $100.00 in outstanding debt to the District of Columbia." The DLCP Business Licensing FAQs state the applicant cannot owe more than $1,000 to DC government. Do not resolve this by guessing; confirm the current threshold with DLCP at (202) 671-4500 before applying.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: BBL Application PDF https://dlcp.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dlcp/publication/attachments/BBL%20Application%20(Fillable).pdf ($100) vs DLCP Business Licensing FAQs https://dlcp.dc.gov/node/1614551 ($1,000) - both loaded 2026-08-17
5. **5. Obtain a Basic Business License (BBL) - CATEGORY/ENDORSEMENT NOT ESTABLISHED** — D.C. Official Code sec. 47-2851.03 authorizes eleven BBL categories: Beauty and grooming services; Charitable services; Contractor and construction services; Entertainment services; Environmental services; Food services; General sales and services; Health services; Housing and lodging services; Public safety services; Vehicular services. The statute does NOT list endorsements - it says DLCP "may create and revise basic business license endorsement categories" by rulemaking and that "such endorsements shall not have a cost to the licensee." IMPORTANT NEGATIVE FINDING: DC's "Vehicular Services" category does NOT cover passenger transport. DLCP defines it as "Repair, servicing, alteration, restoration, towing, painting, cleaning, finishing, or parking of automobiles, trucks, recreational vehicles, boats and other vehicles," and its endorsement list is Auto Rental, Auto Wash, Automobile Repossession Business, Consumer Goods (Auto Repair), Driving School, Motor Vehicle Dealer, New and Used Tire Dealer, Parking Facility, Solid Waste Vehicle, Tow Truck, Tow Truck Business, Tow Truck Storage Lot, Used Car Lot, Used Car Seller Only, Valet Parking. No NEMT, medical transport, ambulance, or vehicle-for-hire endorsement appears. The paper BBL application's License Type checkboxes are Beauty & Grooming, Charitable Services, Emp.-Ind. Salesperson, Employment Services, Entertainment Services, Flammable Product Sales/Services, Food Services, General Business, Health Services, Lodging, Rental Housing, Regulated Business, Other. WHICH BOX AN NEMT COMPANY CHECKS - most plausibly "General Business" under General Sales and Services - IS UNRESOLVED. DLCP's own general-sales-and-services page 404'd for me. Call the Business Licensing Division at (202) 671-4500 / (202) 442-4400 and get the endorsement name in writing.

Official Code sec. 47-2851.03 https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-2851.03 (loaded); DLCP Vehicular Services https://dlcp.dc.gov/page/vehicular-services (loaded); BBL Application PDF (loaded); https://dlcp.dc.gov/page/general-sales-and-services returned HTTP 404 on 2026-08-17
6. **6. Determine that you do NOT need an EMS/ambulance license - and stay inside the exemption** — D.C. Official Code sec. 7-2341.02 (Emergency Medical Services Act of 2008, D.C. Law 17-357, effective March 25, 2009) exempts from the EMS chapter "validly licensed vehicles operated solely for the transportation of non-emergency patients to and from treatment facilities as outpatients," and adds: "this exemption shall not apply to any vehicle which is in any way held out as an emergency medical response vehicle." If you hold yourself out as emergency-capable you fall under the full EMS agency regime, which begins with a CERTIFICATE OF NEED from the State Health Planning and Development Agency (SHPDA) before DC Health will even accept an EMS agency application. THIS IS A MARKETING CONSTRAINT, NOT JUST AN OPERATIONS ONE: a now-superseded version of 29 DCMR sec. 509.2 treated as "conclusive proof" that a vehicle is held out as an ambulance (a) the word "ambulance" on the vehicle, in advertising, or in the company name; (b) dome lights/sirens or similar marking; (c) advertising in an ambulance/emergency listing; and (d) "Any advertising of the vehicle or the company which is designed to inform the public of the availability of the transportation service for non-ambulatory or handicapped patients which does not state in clear, express terms that the vehicle is not equipped to supply emergency service as an ambulance." I could not confirm that clause survives in current law - see the vehicle section - but it is the only DC text I found that speaks directly to NEMT ADVERTISING, and it argues for an explicit "not an ambulance / not equipped for emergency service" disclaimer on the website.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: D.C. Official Code sec. 7-2341.02 https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/7-2341.02 (loaded 2026-08-17); sec. 7-2341.01 definitions https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/7-2341.01 (loaded); DC Health EMS Agency Application Process https://dchealth.dc.gov/page/ems-agency-application-process (loaded); superseded 29 DCMR ch.5 sec. 509 text from the 2009 LexisNexis compilation hosted by DC Health at https://dchealth.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/doh/publication/attachments/Regulation_EMS_29_5_Ambulance%20Service.pdf (downloaded, text-extracted 2026-08-17)
7. **7. Determine whether DFHV (for-hire vehicle) licensing applies - LIKELY NOT, BUT NOT CLEANLY EXEMPTED** — The DC Department of For-Hire Vehicles issues: Driver Licensing; H-Tag / Taxicab Vehicle Registration / Limousine Vehicle Registration; Company Operating Authority; Non-District Limousine (NDL) permit; Special Event Permit. There is NO NEMT, wheelchair-van, or medical-transport license type. D.C. Official Code sec. 50-301.03(17) defines "public vehicle-for-hire" as "A class of transportation service by motor vehicle for hire in the District, including a taxicab, limousine, or sedan-class vehicle" (effective March 10, 2015) - none of which describes a contracted Medicaid wheelchair van. Separately, sec. 50-301.13(f) applies licensing to owners of taxicabs, "an ambulance for hire," funeral vehicles, contract livery, and sightseeing vehicles. I did NOT find an express statutory carve-out in Title 50 for Medicaid NEMT; the express NEMT exemption I found lives in the EMS chapter (sec. 7-2341.02), not the for-hire chapter. Secondary sources also report that dispatch-service rules at sec. 50-301.31 exempt "non-emergency medical transportation arranged through a healthcare provider" - REPORTED, I did not load that section's text. Treat DFHV applicability as an open legal question, not a settled exemption.

### b. Becoming a provider — the path

1. **0. READ THIS FIRST — Maryland has NO single 'become an NEMT provider' front door, and for sedan/wheelchair work there is probably NO Medicaid enrollment step at all** — Maryland's NEMT benefit is delivered by GRANT to 24 county-level grantees who then SUBCONTRACT to transport vendors (COMAR 10.09.19.03 requires the grantee to name its subcontractors and monitor their performance). The path is therefore: form and license the business at the STATE level (SDAT + PSC and/or WMATC and/or MIEMSS), then win a CONTRACT from each county grantee you want to serve — county by county, 24 separate relationships. CRITICAL AND UNRESOLVED: whether an ambulatory/wheelchair NEMT vendor enrolls in Maryland Medicaid at all. MDH transmittal PT 59-26 (Jan 29, 2026) publishes what presents as the COMPLETE list of Maryland Medicaid provider types sorted by screening risk — Attachment 1 (high and moderate risk) and Attachment 2 (limited risk). I read both attachments in full. There is an 'Ambulance Company - PT TI' (limited risk). There is NO sedan, van, wheelchair, 'transportation', or 'NEMT' provider type anywhere in either attachment. Combined with the grant-and-subcontract architecture of COMAR 10.09.19, the strong inference is that ambulatory and wheelchair NEMT vendors are paid by the county grantee out of grant funds and are NOT Medicaid-enrolled billing providers. I am labeling this REPORTED rather than VERIFIED because it rests on ABSENCE from a list plus structural inference, not on an affirmative MDH statement. Confirm with MDH before relying on it.
   - REPORTED · Source: https://health.maryland.gov/mmcp/provider/Documents/transmittals/PT59-26_Provider_Enrollment_Portal_Transition.pdf (PT 59-26, dated January 29, 2026; Attachments 1 and 2 read in full) + https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/10.09.19.03 — both accessed 2026-08-17
2. **1. Form the entity and register with Maryland SDAT** — Register a corporation, LLC, or trade name with the Maryland Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT) and obtain a federal EIN. You must supply your SDAT Department ID number on the PSC carrier application, and if filing as a corporation or LLC you must attach the Articles of Incorporation/Organization AND a current Certificate of Good Standing from SDAT. If you use a trade name it must be filed with SDAT and a copy attached. MIEMSS separately requires an EIN and SDAT registration for ambulance licensure (COMAR 30.09.04.03).
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://psc.maryland.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/Form-28-Carrier-Application-Packet-Revised-9-26-23.pdf (PSC Form 28 packet, revised 9-26-23, 'INFORMATION REQUIRED FOR FILING YOUR APPLICATION TO BECOME A CARRIER') + https://www.miemss.org/home/Portals/0/Docs/CommercialAmbulance/Commercial_Amb_FAQ.pdf — accessed 2026-08-17

4. **3. CHECK THE GOVERNMENT-CONTRACT EXEMPTIONS BEFORE YOU FILE — Maryland statute may exempt an exclusively-NEMT operation from BOTH the carrier permit and the driver licence** — TWO express statutory exemptions bear directly on an NEMT operator working under a county health department contract. (a) CARRIER PERMIT — Md. Public Utilities §9-201(b)(7) exempts 'a motor vehicle used by a privately owned transportation company exclusively to provide transportation system services under a contract with the governing body of a county or municipal corporation or with a unit of State government'. The statute further specifies that such a company must still obtain permits for any vehicles NOT used exclusively for the contracted services. (b) DRIVER LICENCE — Md. Public Utilities §10-102(b)(2) exempts 'transportation solely provided by or on behalf of a unit of federal, State, or local government, or a nonprofit organization as identified in §501(c)(3) and (4)' that conducts criminal history and driving record checks, for clients of listed services WHICH EXPRESSLY INCLUDE THE 'Medical Assistance Program' (alongside aging, disabilities, kidney dialysis, Head Start, Welfare-to-Work, mental health, job training). BUT §10-102(c) slams the door on mixed operations: 'Subsection (b)(2) of this section may not be construed to limit the application of this title or Title 9 of this article to a for-hire driver or other person who operates a motor vehicle for hire or provides transportation of persons for hire in addition to providing transportation services to clients of services listed in subsection (b)(2).' PRACTICAL READ: a company that does NOTHING but county-contract Medicaid NEMT may be exempt from PSC carrier authority and from PSC for-hire driver licensing; the moment it takes ANY private-pay or other for-hire work, the exemptions collapse and full PSC compliance attaches. The word 'exclusively' in §9-201(b)(7) and 'solely' in §10-102(b)(2) are doing enormous work. Note also that a Maryland local health department is a hybrid state/county entity, so whether an LHD subcontract is a contract 'with the governing body of a county' or 'with a unit of State government' — and whether NEMT is 'transportation system services' — is genuinely ambiguous on the face of the statute. GET A LEGAL OPINION; DO NOT SELF-CERTIFY INTO THESE EXEMPTIONS.

10. **9. THE REAL GATE — win a contract from each county grantee, one jurisdiction at a time** — This is where Maryland NEMT is actually won or lost, and it is the county-level variation problem in its sharpest form. There is NO uniform statewide vendor standard: COMAR 10.09.19 imposes NO vehicle specification, NO driver qualification, NO insurance minimum, and NO training requirement on transportation vendors. COMAR 10.09.19.04 sets only screening, referral and cost-effectiveness duties on the GRANTEE, and its transport-mode language does not specify vendor standards. Every substantive vendor requirement therefore lives in an individual county's solicitation and contract, and 24 jurisdictions write those independently. Observed mechanics: solicitations are posted on eMMA (eMaryland Marketplace Advantage, emma.maryland.gov) and/or on the county health department's own site; award structures differ (Talbot County: bidders 'may submit proposals and bids for one, two, or all three transportation service types' — ambulatory, wheelchair, ambulance — and 'TCHD reserves the right to make multiple awards'); terms are typically one fiscal year with option years (Washington County 2026-02 NEMT: July 1 2026 - June 30 2027 plus two optional one-year extensions); and experience thresholds can be steep (Washington County reportedly required at least six years of transportation experience within the past eight years — REPORTED via a secondary mirror, not read in the primary document). Rates are NEGOTIATED LOCALLY, not set on a state fee schedule: the 2019 JCR states local health departments are responsible for 'negotiating rates with local transportation providers', and reported average cost per trip in FY19 ranged from $17.08 (Cecil) to $126.23 (Baltimore County) — a better-than-7x spread across jurisdictions.

### a. Program structure

- **Administering agency:** Virginia Department of Medical Assistance Services (DMAS), 600 East Broad Street, Richmond, Virginia 23219. - **Delivery model today:** HYBRID - a single statewide FFS broker running alongside fully MCO-delegated transportation, with the great majority of volume on the managed care side. Managed care serves over 90% of Virginia Medicaid members (REPORTED, secondary). (3) NOT county-based - there is no county-administered layer. (4) A crucial consequence of this model: DMAS does NOT enroll NEMT providers at all. The broker is the gatekeeper - it credentials, trains, inspects, contracts, sets rates, assigns trips, and pays. DMAS's role is to set minimum program requirements that the broker must enforce and to audit the broker. Exception: air, emergency ground, and non-emergency ground AMBULANCE providers DO enroll with DMAS directly before enrolling with an MCO. ### b. Becoming a provider — the path

1. **1. Form a Virginia business entity with the State Corporation Commission (SCC)** — File Articles of Organization (LLC) or incorporation documents through the SCC Clerk's Information System (CIS) at cis.scc.virginia.gov. Annual registration fee for an LLC is "$50" per year, due "by the last day of the month in which it was formed"; a "$25 penalty will be imposed" if late, and "the LLC's existence will be automatically canceled by operation of law" if unpaid by the last day of the third month after the due date. Stock/nonstock corporations pay a separate annual registration fee with a late penalty "equal to 10% of the fee (but not less than $10)." NOTE: the $100 Articles of Organization formation fee is widely reported but I could NOT confirm it from an SCC primary page (the SCC fee schedule and cis.scc.virginia.gov are JavaScript-rendered and returned no substantive text) - the $50 annual figure IS primary-source verified.
   - REPORTED · Source: Annual $50 fee VERIFIED: https://www.scc.virginia.gov/businesses/maintaining-your-business/ (accessed 2026-08-17). $100 formation fee REPORTED only (secondary: bizreport.com, llcuniversity.com, statebusinesscompliance.com; SCC primary page did not render).
2. **2. Obtain a Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN) from the IRS** — Virginia DMV lists as a required document: "Federal Employer ID Number (FEIN) verification from the IRS, if applicable" for the NEMT carrier certificate application.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/businesses/motor-carriers/intrastate/medical (accessed 2026-08-17)
3. **3. Obtain a Virginia DMV Non-Emergency Medical Transportation Carrier CERTIFICATE OF FITNESS (this IS required - it is a statutory precondition)** — Code of Virginia § 46.2-2099.44 (Article 14, Nonemergency Medical Transportation Carriers): "No nonemergency medical transportation carrier, unless otherwise exempted, shall transport passengers on any highway within the Commonwealth on an intrastate basis without first having obtained from the Department a certificate of fitness authorizing such operation." (2011, cc. 881, 889). The DMV authority document type is a "Certificate." Required documents: Form OA 151; OA 435 Bond or OA 447 Letter of Credit; OA 210 (for each business official). DMV defines the carrier class as "A person who only provides non-emergency medical transportation for Medicaid recipients whose trips are arranged either: Through the Department of Medical Assistance Services (DMAS) or Through a broker operating under a contract with DMAS or Through a broker operating as a Medicaid Managed Care Organization or through a contractor of a Medicaid Managed Care Organization contracted with DMAS." Operating restriction: "May only transport Medicaid recipients whose trips are arranged through DMAS or a transportation broker contracted with DMAS" and "May only be compensated through Medicaid reimbursement." Fees: "$50 filing fee", "$3 operating authority registration fee", "$3 for a duplicate certificate." NOTE: Article 14 as published on Virginia Law contains ONLY § 46.2-2099.44 - there is no NEMT-specific insurance statute; insurance flows from the general Article 2 provisions.
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title46.2/chapter20/article14/ and https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/businesses/motor-carriers/intrastate/medical (both accessed 2026-08-17)
4. **4. Post the $25,000 surety bond or irrevocable letter of credit with DMV** — "You must keep a $25,000 surety bond or letter of credit on file with DMV's Motor Carrier Services for three years from the date your operating authority certificate is issued. DMV's bond-requirements table confirms $25,000 for "3 years from the date the certificate is issued" for NEMT carriers specifically (taxicab, property carrier, employee hauler and non-profit passenger carriers require "none").
   - **VERIFIED** · Source: https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/businesses/motor-carriers/intrastate/medical and https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/businesses/motor-carriers/intrastate/bond-require (accessed 2026-08-17)
5. **5. File proof of insurance with DMV Motor Carrier Services and keep it continuously on file** — Code of Virginia § 46.2-2053: "All motor carriers shall keep in force at all times insurance, a bond or bonds, in an amount required by this section." § 46.2-2054 requires policies or surety bonds to be filed with the Department. DMV states a certificate or permit may be revoked if proof of insurance is not kept on file. See the insuranceRequirements section for exact amounts.

## 4. THE TWO CONTESTED FIGURES — verdicts

Both figures the owner reported were tested against primary sources by two independent research passes (a
per-jurisdiction pass and a dedicated cross-cutting pass).

### 4a. The \$1.5M insurance minimum — **CONFIRMED, with caveats and three candidate origins**

**CONFIRMED as a published requirement of MTM Health, the DC fee-for-service broker.** MTM's *DC NET
Transportation Provider Frequently Asked Questions* answers "What kind of insurance coverage does my company
need to have?" with: **"general and automobile liability policies of no less than \$1,500,000 each"**, plus
workers' compensation meeting state requirements, and MTM named as Certificate Holder **and** Additional
Insured on both policies. The document is linked from MTM's official DC program page and was live
(HTTP 200) and text-extracted on 2026-08-17. — **VERIFIED**

**Three caveats that keep this off our site as-is:**

1. **The document carries no revision date**; its upload path is `2013/01`.
2. **MTM's own archived DC-titled handbook contradicts it** at *"Vehicular liability of no less than
   \$500,000 Combined Single Limit"* and \$500,000 CGL aggregate (footer: "Standard: Revised 8.22.13"). Its
   live URL now redirects to MTM's homepage; text recovered from an Internet Archive snapshot. **Do not
   resolve this by assuming the higher or newer-looking number is current.**
3. **MTM's other-state pages say \$1,000,000** ("each" for general and auto liability; Missouri \$2M).

**Two independent alternative origins for a "\$1.5M in this market" belief, both real:**

- **Virginia DMV / Code of Virginia § 46.2-2053** sets **\$1,500,000** for a vehicle designed to carry
  **7 to 15 passengers including the driver** — i.e. a typical wheelchair van or cutaway. — **VERIFIED**
- **WMATC Regulation No. 58** sets **\$1,500,000 CSL** for holders of a *restricted* Certificate of Authority
  (vehicles seating fewer than 16 including the driver) in the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit District.
  — REPORTED

**A name-collision warning worth recording:** Washington **State** (not Washington, DC) also uses a
\$1,500,000 CSL for auto transportation carrying 15 or fewer passengers. Searches for "Washington \$1.5M NEMT
insurance" will surface it. — **VERIFIED, and out of scope.**

**An internal disagreement, recorded rather than hidden:** the cross-cutting pass concluded that MTM publishes
**no** DC dollar figure, while the DC pass found and downloaded the FAQ that states \$1.5M. The reconciliation
is a coverage difference, not a factual conflict — MTM's `robots.txt` disallows crawling DOC/PDF assets (a
constraint already recorded in `docs/DESIGN_RESEARCH.md`), so the figure is reachable only by fetching the
linked `.docx` directly, which one pass did and the other did not.

### 4b. Virginia CSL \$300,000–\$1,000,000 — **CORRECTED**

**The Virginia state floor for a 1-to-6-passenger NEMT vehicle (sedan, minivan, most wheelchair vans) is
\$350,000** for Bodily Injury and Property Damage combined — Code of Virginia **§ 46.2-2053**, enforced by
Virginia DMV as a condition of NEMT carrier operating authority. — **VERIFIED**

- **\$350,000 falls inside the recalled range but matches neither endpoint.** \$300,000 corresponds to nothing
  in Virginia law located by either pass; \$1,000,000 likewise — though a broker requirement above the state
  floor is the likely origin, and that remains `UNRESOLVED` (no public Modivcare page states DC/MD/VA limits).
- **Virginia never uses the term "combined single limit."** DMV's own column heading is "Bodily Injury and
  Property Damage" and the statute says "minimum financial responsibility requirement." It *functions* as a
  CSL — one figure covering both — but **no primary source labels it one**, so our copy must not either.
- **The full Virginia tier structure** (all **VERIFIED**, § 46.2-2053): 1–6 passengers **\$350,000**; 7–15
  passengers **\$1,500,000**; 16+ passengers **\$5,000,000**; taxicabs **\$125,000**. Plus a **\$25,000 surety
  bond or irrevocable letter of credit**, held three years from certificate issuance.
- **DMAS sets no independent dollar figure** and defers entirely to DMV — for taxis and multi-passenger vans,
  "the greater of the amount required by the Virginia DMV or the city or county ordinance for taxis." So a
  **local ordinance can raise the floor**, which no single statewide number can capture. — **VERIFIED**
