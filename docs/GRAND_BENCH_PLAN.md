## Why this is its own file

`DESIGN_RESEARCH.md` is a ~2,700-line append-only archive of findings; `SITE_BUILD_PLAN.md` is the
phase/workstream map. This roster is neither. It is an **executable build order for one task**, meant
to be worked from directly and checked off, and it has a different lifecycle from both: research is
history and never changes, the build plan is stable across many tasks, and this document is consumed
and closed by Task #30. Burying a build order at line 2,700 of an archive would make it hard to work
from and would corrupt the archive's append-only character. The workstream files link here; this file
links back. Same reasoning that keeps the QA harness in `scripts/qa/` rather than in prose.

## What the bench is for

**D27 is a ruling of intent, not a set of instructions.** The owner's verdict was that the build is
~85% right but **too quiet** — flat nav, mute background lines, list-shaped navigation, newspaper
feel. Every directive that follows from that is a *direction*, and directions have to be **seen** to be
judged. The bench is where they become visible.

**The protocol is `nexo-motion` §4, unchanged:**

1. One self-contained page, opened from `file://`, no build step.
2. Identical fragments across candidates — only the candidate layer varies.
3. Every candidate shown in **both motion states**: motion-on and reduced-motion, side by side.
4. **Budgets measured and printed on the page** — element counts, layer counts, painted-pixel contrast
   — never asserted in prose.
5. **The flagship surface is included**, not only the flattering one.
6. The owner picks. *"None of these"* and *"quieter"* are legitimate answers.
7. The pick becomes the implementation spec for a separate full-cube task.

**Nothing ships from a bench.**

### Laws that bind every candidate without exception

- **D23 perception floors** — and floors-only-rise. A bolder treatment may not spend a floor.
- **D20 performance bar** — ≤600KB transfer, ≤150KB JS, ≤1200ms DCL, ≤60 requests. A ceiling, not a
  target. §15d is the receipt that the patterns worth taking are the cheap ones.
- **Motion-safe architecture** (`nexo-motion` §1.1) — motion only inside a sanctioned scope. The
  structural guard will fail the build otherwise; a bench fragment that could not pass it is not a
  candidate.
- **Static-complete** (§1.2) — with the motion scope deleted, every fragment still renders finished.
- **Decline-don't-degrade** (D20b) — no cheap version of an effect that will not run well.
- **Zero CLS.**
- **Status-semantics law** — jade owns interaction and status; D25 warmth is **atmosphere, semantically
  silent**, and may never carry meaning or enter an alarm's colour space.
- **The copy gate (§7)** — every string, including anything a diagram labels.
- **D27h, the intentionality doctrine** — **for each element in each candidate, one sentence of
  purpose.** If the sentence is "it looks nice", the element is cut. This is a bench deliverable, not a
  disposition: the sentences get written on the page.

### Sequencing — and why the hero is deliberately last

**W2, the hero signature piece, comes AFTER this bench — and it is THE NEXT DESIGN TASK once the owner has ruled** (reaffirmed 2026-08-21). D27c raises its priority, and the temptation
is to build the most exciting thing first. That would be the same mistake as choosing a colour before
testing the ink register: the signature piece has to **speak the braid (B3) and ambient (B4) language**,
and those are what the bench establishes. Building it first freezes a vocabulary nobody has chosen.

**Fragments to render.**

  - *N2 — non-ink direction:* **the one D27a explicitly opens.** A bar that is not black/ink — a deep

- **ONE Railway-class open panel, built for the REAL Platform menu** — no lorem, no placeholder
  destinations. The four real ones, with their existing approved copy:

  | Destination | Copy already written (`src/lib/nav.ts`) |
  |---|---|
  | **Dispatch** | *Assign and route every trip from one queue.* |
  | **Claims & billing** | *Turn completed trips into clean, submittable claims.* |
  | **Compliance** | *Keep credentials, signatures, and audit trails in order.* |
  | **Oversight & reporting** | *Exception review, pattern reports, and CSV/Excel exports.* |

  Built on the §15b mechanic: a panel **darker than the page**, cells filled at the **page colour**
  with a small radius and **no border**, one **featured cell** promoted with our own line-art
  iconography, the rest as rows. Iconography is **inline SVG in our existing line language** — not an
  imported illustration asset (§15b, verdict 7).

**Interaction to demonstrate.** Trigger → open; **trigger → trigger with `nexo-shape`**, the panel
interpolating its height between destinations (§15b measured Railway at 266→302px over ~120ms);
cell hover **and its focus twin**; the whole thing under reduced motion, where the panel snaps and
every cell is complete.

**Laws that bind.** `--nav-glass` alpha 0.90 was calibrated for dark glass over pure white — **any new
surface re-derives every on-nav text ratio from scratch**. A page-coloured cell on a darker panel is a

both survive whatever ships — and note that **I21 stays the floor even though D27 supersedes the
hairline question**. `overflow: hidden` on a morphing panel **will clip a focus ring** — verify focus
inside the morph, not only at rest. One `<nav>` landmark: the boxed grid lives inside the existing
Radix panel, it does not become a second landmark.

**What the owner will judge.** Does the closed bar read as **present** without reading as heavy? Is a
non-ink nav still *Nexo*, or does it break the identity? Does a panel of boxes feel like a designed
object rather than a menu? And the blunt one: **is it still too quiet?**

## B2 — MOBILITY CARDS

**Purpose.** D27d, and **the concrete form of S-001** — the owner's *"cards that move, a lot of
animation but not noisy, implemented smartly"* finally has a subject. The three service levels as
**beautiful switching cards** in the GitLab dark-card register (§14d).

**Fragments to render.** The card set, real content, no placeholders:

| Level | Line |
|---|---|
| **Ambulatory** | walks with minimal assistance |
| **Wheelchair** | seated, secured for transport |
| **Stretcher** | transported lying down |

Plus **bariatric & two-person assist rendered as what it is: a MODIFIER that layers on any of the
three** — a band, a toggle, a chip on each card. **Never a fourth card.** Render two card
architectures if the switching interaction differs between them (e.g. a stacked deck vs. a three-up
set where one is active).

**Interaction to demonstrate.** The **switch** — how a card becomes active and the others recede. This
is the whole point of the candidate. Show the transition at real speed and stepped, plus the
reduced-motion state where the active card is simply *already* active.

**Laws that bind.** **THE LEVEL COUNT IS LAW: three, and assist is a modifier** (`nexo-brand` §7.1). A
four-card set is a copy-gate violation, not a design variation. **Never claim platform enforcement** of
assist/bariatric — it is an operational offering. **Dignity language is mandatory**: needs-based,
never size-as-spectacle, never clinical-gawking. Service-level colours are a **dedicated palette
outside the status hues** and always pair colour with icon + label — never colour alone. **Zero CLS:**
a switching set must not resize its container. **≤300ms, transform/opacity, never from `scale(0)`,
read-first copy not animated.** S-001's hard part carries over verbatim: **"not noisy" is a design
problem, not a volume knob** — density of craft, not of movement.

**What the owner will judge.** Do the cards feel *rich* without feeling busy? Does the switch feel
like one object changing state, or three objects fighting? Does the modifier read as a modifier?

## B3 — BRAIDED ROUTE

> **AMENDED BY S-027 (owner, 2026-08-21) — and the amendment RESOLVES the open question rather
> than reopening it.** The directive: the braid is **scaled to be seen**, the strands are
> **genuinely interwoven** (*"a tangle of snakes"*), and **the van rides the weave serpentine /
> roller-coaster style, passing OVER some strands and BEHIND others**. The braid is **"a platform
> for the van to move around"**.
>
> **RECONCILIATION WITH THE #29 CANONICAL-STRAND CALL — they agree.** #29 recommended one strand
> stay canonical so the van's 256-point LUT samples a single path. That still holds, with one
> word changed: **the canonical path IS the weaving path.** The van rides ONE path, sampled
> exactly as it is today; that path is now serpentine and passes through the braid instead of
> running beside it. **Nothing about the LUT architecture changes** — same single surface, same
> one-path sampling, same per-frame `setAttribute`.
>
> **OVER/UNDER IS PAINT ORDER, NOT GEOMETRY.** The illusion of the van weaving through strands is
> produced by **segmenting the strands** and drawing the segments that should cross in FRONT of
> the van **after** the van group in document order. Segments that pass behind are drawn before.
> No z-index, no second raster surface, no compositing trick — **Task #22's single-surface law is
> preserved**, which is what makes this safe to attempt at all.
>
> **I20 KEEPS ITS MEANING EXACTLY.** The invariant asserts *the van rides the line*; it still does.
> The line got interesting. **The invariant at real risk is I15**, decorative overlap: a braid
> scaled to be seen is wider than a hairline, and I15 asserts zero overlap with text and the
> footer. **B3 must print the width budget**, not assume it.

**Purpose.** D27f — evolve the single route line into a **braided multi-strand motif**, weaving the
D25 rose/violet with the jade lead.

**Fragments to render.**

- **Two to three static braid geometries**, each at **two scales**: **hero scale** (a short, thick,
  legible braid where the strands are individually readable) and **route scale** (the full page-length
  spine, where a braid must survive being 1–2px per strand). *The second scale is where braids usually
  die, and it is the one that matters.*
- **ONE motion sketch** of the drawing/flow behaviour — how the strands draw in, and whether they
  draw together, offset, or with a lead.

**Laws that bind — this is the most invariant-protected object on the site.**
**I20** asserts the van rides the line, across zoom and DPR legs in all three engines. **I15** asserts
zero decorative overlap with text and with the footer. A braid changes the geometry both measure.
**The LUT architecture decides feasibility** (`nexo-motion` §2.2): the van is positioned from a
256-point table sampled off **one** path. **The strongly recommended framing is that ONE strand stays
canonical** — the van rides the jade lead, the others are decorative — because the alternative is
re-deriving the LUT against a braid, which is a different and much larger task. **D25 binds the
colour: rose and violet are semantically silent.** A strand that *carries meaning* would give warmth a
job, which D25 forbids — strands are visual, not semantic. **Perf:** N strands is N× path length drawn
per frame; `stroke-dashoffset` on several long paths is not free, and the bench must print the number.

**What the owner will judge.** Does the braid read as one motif or as three lines that happen to be
near each other? Does it survive at route scale? Does the warmth belong, or does it look applied?

**Purpose.** D27e — the background must read as an **intentional system model**: aggressive, legible,
semantic. **Never unexplained decoration.** This is a **redesign** of the ambient layer, not a tuning
of it; the current `AmbientMap` is deliberately abstract, and that is precisely what the ruling
rejects.

**Fragments to render.** **Two directions**, each rendered **behind a real content band** — real

- *A1 — the region made legible:* the DC/MD/VA map stops being a suggestion and becomes a **map**:

- *A2 — the dispatch-network model:* our equivalent of Railway's product canvas (§15c) — trips, stops,

Each direction ships **its own legend of intent**: for every kind of line and node, one sentence of
what it means (D27h).

**Laws that bind.** **Text sovereignty is not negotiable** (`nexo-brand` §6.1): a more legible

**What the owner will judge.** Can he look at any line and know what it means? Is it aggressive enough
to register, and quiet enough to read over? Does it look like the product, or like an illustration of

## Craft amendments — from `emil-design-eng` and `frontend-design`

Both skills were run against the roster after it was drafted. These are the changes they produced,
kept as their own section so the reasoning trail stays visible rather than being silently folded in.

### The one that is about code we have already shipped

**Our nav panel animates with `@keyframes`, and trigger→trigger is exactly the case keyframes handle
worst.** `globals.css` drives the panel with `animation: navPanelIn` / `navPanelOut` off Radix
`data-state`. **Keyframes restart from zero when re-triggered; CSS transitions retarget from their
current value.** Moving quickly along a menu bar re-triggers the panel repeatedly, which is the
textbook case for transitions. **B1 should build its panel on transitions, not keyframes**, and if the
bench pick ships, the existing keyframe pair is replaced rather than extended. *(This is a finding
about the current build, not about the candidate — filed here because B1 is where it gets fixed.)*

### `transition: all` — where we can beat the reference outright

Railway's destination cells declare **`transition: all`** (measured, §15b). That is an anti-pattern:
it animates every property that happens to change, including ones that trigger layout, and it makes
the intent unreadable. **Our cells name exact properties** — `background-color`, and nothing else
unless a property earns its place. This is small, concrete, and it is a real answer to the
credit-and-improve obligation in §15g: we are not copying their CSS, we are copying their idea and
declaring it properly.

### Motion specifics that bind the bench fragments

| Amendment | Applies to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Name exact transition properties; never `transition: all` | B1, B2 | Readable intent; no accidental layout animation |
| Panel morph curve is an **open bench question**, not settled | B1 | An element *reshaping on screen* argues for `ease-in-out`; Railway ships an ease-out (`cubic-bezier(0,0,.2,1)`). Render both and let the eye decide |
| Gate hover behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` | B1, B2 | Touch devices fire hover on tap, so an ungated hover state sticks after a tap |
| Exit faster than enter | B1, B2 | Asymmetry reads as responsive; symmetric timing reads as sluggish on the way out |
| `:active { transform: scale(0.97) }` on anything pressable | B1, B2 | Press feedback is the cheapest "the interface heard you" signal there is |
| Try `filter: blur(2px)` **during** the card crossfade | B2 | When a crossfade shows two overlapping states, a brief blur bridges them into one transformation. `filter` is already permitted by `nexo-brand` §5 |
| Stagger stays **30–80ms** | B1, B2 | Our nav cascade is already 45ms — inside the band, no change needed |
| Keep `transform-origin` on the trigger, not centre | B1 | Already `nexo-brand` law; restated because a morphing panel makes it easy to lose |

### The frequency test, and the sharp question it puts to `nexo-drift`

The decision framework starts with *how often will users see this?* — and the answer for an **ambient
background animation is "continuously, on every page, for as long as they are reading."** That is past
the far end of the scale where the guidance is to reduce or remove. **B5 must therefore answer a
question it did not previously have to:** does a drifting background survive being seen *constantly*,
or does it become the thing you notice instead of the content? Render drift **beside** a static
version of the same ambient, at the same size, and judge them together. **"Static wins" is a
legitimate and likely outcome**, and B4's redesign may make drift redundant — a background that means
something may not need to move to earn attention.

### Boldness has a budget, and D27 lists eight directives

*Spend your boldness in one place.* D27 asks for a bolder nav **and** bolder cards **and** a braided
route **and** an aggressive ambient **and** a leading hero animation. **If every candidate wins, the
page has five signatures and therefore none.** The bench should surface this deliberately: alongside
the individual candidates, show **one composite frame** — bold nav + new ambient + cards in a single
viewport — so the owner is choosing a *page*, not five isolated wins. **The likely correct answer is
that one candidate is the signature and the others are disciplined support**, and that is an owner
call the bench should make easy to see rather than discover after implementation.

### The "newspaper feel" verdict lands on a known default — which independently corroborates D27

Current AI-generated design clusters around three recognisable looks, and one of them is
**"broadsheet layout, hairline rules, zero border-radius, dense columns."** The owner's own words for
the current build were **"list-shaped navigation, newspaper feel."** He arrived at that verdict by
eye, with no reference to this taxonomy — and it names the same failure mode.

**That is worth recording, because it changes what D27 means.** The site has not merely drifted quiet;
it has drifted toward a **default**, and defaults are what the whole project exists to avoid. It also

### Bench protocol additions

- **Slow-motion and frame-by-frame.** Every motion fragment ships a control to play it at 0.2× and
  step it. Timing faults between coordinated properties are invisible at full speed.
- **Judge the next day.** Fresh eyes catch what the building eye cannot. Not a formality: the bench is
  a taste instrument, and taste is worse when tired.
- **Real device for anything touched.** Hover gating and press feedback cannot be judged with a mouse.
