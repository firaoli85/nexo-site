Your concept studies said the signature should read as complexity with intent, and connected it to the rod of Asclepius — which gives the braid a stated meaning it did not have before: a medical symbol rebuilt as a routing diagram. S1 — GEOMETRY A: all-weave cables

shipped geometry, re-stranded
What changed and what did not. The paths are the shipped Task #35 geometry, unaltered: swing 122 at 1440, wavelength 433, phases 0 and ±2π/3. Only the stroke treatment changed — each strand became a ribbon cable. That matters for the law: cables are a rendering of the SAME paths, so the canonical-path law is untouched and the LUT still samples one lead.
THE D29 INTERPRETIVE LINE, and it needs saying out loud: the lead is drawn as 4 mint fibers but it is ONE semantic mint object. Multi-fiber rendering does not multiply "active routes" — there is still exactly one live line, and mint restraint still permits exactly two mint surfaces on the noir register.
The texture echo is line-art only. Companions carry a micro-dash sheen fiber (2.2/3.4) — the cable catching light. That is the only survivor of the photoreal mesh in your images 2–3; the mesh itself is rejected for chrome (see the rejection note under S2).
Clearance: the cable's fiber spread counts toward strand extent. At the shipped spacing the bundle is ~4.2px wide, so the outermost fiber sits ~2.1px beyond the path. measured against the 28.4px minimum the shipped route holds, the cable spends 2.1 of 28.4px and the 1.39:1 text law is not approached.

S2 — GEOMETRY B: staff and coil (Asclepius)

D27h — the braid states its meaning
The construction. The lead runs calm (swing 26, a long wavelength) and sits centred inside the coil. The companions carry the amplitude: swing 122 at wavelength 270, i.e. tighter and larger. The staff is still; the serpent moves around it.
THE CONSTRAINT, STATED HONESTLY: staff-and-coil buys NO extra amplitude, and it needs no extra room either. The same left budget binds both geometries at 122px at 1440 and 140 at 1920, because the text sits to the right and the swing opens left. So S2 is not "bigger", it is a redistribution: S1 spends the budget across three equal strands, S2 gives nearly all of it to the companions and almost none to the lead. What that buys is contrast of motion, not size.
What it costs. A calm lead is a calm active line, and the active line is the thing the reader is supposed to follow. S2 makes the inactive strands the energetic ones, which is the opposite emphasis from D29's accent law, even though the colour hierarchy is untouched. That is a real trade and it is the owner's to rule.
REJECTED FOR CHROME — the photoreal mesh (your images 2–3). Reasons, not taste: D19/D20 (a raster mesh or 3D road surface is a payload and a paint cost the route budget does not have), the medium collision (every other mark on this site is line art, and one photoreal object would read as a foreign asset rather than as the signature), and it cannot be register-split: a photoreal surface cannot invert across the ink/paper seam the way a stroke can. It survives here only as the micro-dash sheen.
S2-ink staff & coil, ink register
Ink. The coil crossings are far more dramatic than S1's, and the lead is noticeably quieter.
S2-light staff & coil, light register
Light. Same trade, and more visible here: the charcoal coil is the loudest thing in the gutter.

S3 — LIVERY: the vehicle on a crossing

D33 ruling is open to challenge
All three sit on the same cable crossing, at the same size, with the under-glow live. Smallness is preserved on purpose: your prompt called it a delicate UI element, and a van scaled up to show its livery is a van answering a different question.
THE MEASUREMENT, and it refuted what I expected to write. A vehicle needs a silhouette on BOTH registers, and every one of these three has exactly one register where its body fails — they just fail on OPPOSITE ones. (a) and (b) read on paper (body 17.3:1) and are invisible on ink (1.00:1), so the edge carries them there, and it carries them hard, at 11.6:1 and 18.6:1. (c) is the reverse: the silver body reads on ink (11.71:1) but nearly vanishes on paper (1.48:1), where its darker edge has to rescue it at only 3.74:1. So the intuition that a solid body is the safe choice is wrong: silver is the candidate with the weakest rescue on the register it fails.
S-007 binds regardless of prompt wording: any glow is white or mint (155°). Cyan stays rejected.
