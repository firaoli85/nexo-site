Cable texture

What is being varied, and what is not. The geometry is the shipped staff-and-coil at real route scale: coil swing 122 at wavelength 270, staff swing 26. Only the texture on the dark coil cables changes. The mint staff is never textured — it is the live line, and a textured active route reads as a damaged one.

The technique, and the one that was rejected. The shipped texture is the pattern used directly as stroke paint on a second pass over each fiber, with the pattern's own marks painted in the register-aware strand gradient — so the ink/paper flip happens inside the texture for free. The first build used a mask instead, and it was a D20 violation caught by measurement: a mask forces its subtree to rasterise over the mask's whole region, here a 4856px-tall route, and the van's per-frame transform dirtied it every frame. Measured 465ms median frames against 1.8ms with the texture off. Pattern-as-stroke measures 0.10ms/frame, which is inside the noise of a 16.7ms vsync floor.

The honest limitation: an SVG <pattern> tiles in user space, so the mesh does not follow each strand's local direction. At this scale that reads as material grain rather than as a mistake, and the strands are static geometry so nothing slides underneath it on scroll.

And the honest range limit: on the INK register the companions sit at N1-A's ruled 1.66:1 recession, and at that contrast there is almost nothing for a sub-pixel mesh to modulate. The texture is effectively invisible on ink at dpr 1, on every one of these six. Raising ink recession to make it show would break N1-A. The texture is a light-register quality and ink does not get it.

C3, the cheap-monitor risk: every candidate is shot at dpr 1 and dpr 2. A texture tuned on a retina panel can vanish or turn to dirt on a 1× display, which is most of the people reading this site.
