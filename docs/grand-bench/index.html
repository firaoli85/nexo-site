The Grand Bench

Five candidates for the D27 Boldness Ruling, rendered so they can be judged rather than argued about. Nothing here ships. Every fragment models future site law (real tokens, real copy, motion-safe architecture), but a candidate is a proposal until you rule on it. React by B-number: adopt, adapt + direction, or reject. “None of these” and “quieter” are legitimate answers.

THE “FIVE SIGNATURES = NONE” CHECK, answered honestly.
They do not all survive together, and the composite is where that becomes obvious. Scrolled as one page, the ink-bold nav, the braid and the cards are three strong voices in the first two screens, and the braid is the one that wins the fight, because it is the largest moving object on the page and it sits directly beside the headline. That is a problem if the headline is the message, and it is the answer if the braid IS the signature.

What I would tell you to decide first: whether the braid is the site's signature or the route's decoration. If it is the signature, B3 goes to hero scale and the nav should get quieter, not bolder: B1(iii) fill-against-fill, not B1(i) ink-bold. If it is decoration, the braid stays at route scale in the margin, and B1(i) is right.

What genuinely coexists: the ambient (B4-d2) and everything else. It is the only candidate that got louder without getting louder, because it reads as structure rather than as an effect, and text sits on it cleanly (7.51:1). B2 also coexists, because its motion is bounded to a click.

What I would cut from this composite: the braid at hero scale and the ink-bold nav together. Pick one. Chanel's rule applies: before leaving the house, take one thing off.
