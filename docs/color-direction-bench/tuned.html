How the numbers below were produced

Every ratio marked MEASURED comes from sampling the actual painted pixels of this page behind the text in question, not from compositing the alphas by hand. That is a Task #24 lesson made law by D25: the first bench scored text against a theoretical sun core and unfairly failed a direction whose text sits in the falloff. Where a value is a plain token pairing with no glow beneath it, it is computed directly and labelled without the MEASURED tag.

What the ink hero found, and it changes the implementation spec

A glow on the ink register necessarily costs the ink card its FILL separation, and that is arithmetic rather than a tuning error. Pure ink sits at luminance 0.00651 and the D23 B06 floor of 1.25 against --ink-surface permits at most 0.00649. Any usable glow lifts the ink past that, so the shipped --ink-surface #192b24 measures 1.07 / 1.08 / 1.02 against the painted ink in T1 / T2 / T3 instead of 1.25.

The bench fails that line rather than excusing it, because D25 says atmosphere may never push a delta below its floor. The resolution is not to delete the glow but to COMPENSATE the pair, and the bench prints the compensated numbers directly above:

· --ink-surface #192b24 → #273a32 (holds 1.32 / 1.34 / 1.25)
· --on-ink-border-strong #5b8275 → #688f82 (the lifted surface would otherwise drop the card edge to 2.82, under its 3:1 tier; #688f82 restores it to 3.36)

These are candidate values, not tokens. They belong to the warm-atmosphere implementation task, which re-verifies them with the full cube. This is exactly the kind of thing the flagship fragment existed to find: the light register hid it completely, because a light page has luminance headroom to spare and ink has almost none.
